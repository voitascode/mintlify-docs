title: "What to know before a new website"
description: "Five decisions that are cheap to make now and expensive to change once design has started."
---

A new website fails for boring reasons far more often than creative ones. Here are the five things worth settling before anyone opens a design tool — each one cheap now, expensive at step seven.

## 1. Know why you're rebuilding

"It looks dated" is a symptom, not a goal. A redesign that fixes only the appearance tends to produce the same business results in a nicer font.

Pin down what should measurably change: more qualified enquiries, shorter sales cycles, fewer support emails, better hiring applications. That answer drives the information architecture, the copy, and what we measure afterwards.

<Warning>
  If nobody can name a metric that should move, the project has no definition of success — and every later disagreement becomes a matter of taste.
</Warning>

## 2. Decide who decides

The single most common cause of delay is not design disagreement. It's ambiguity about who has final say.

You need one person who can approve a direction. Others can review and comment — that's healthy — but design by committee produces an average of everyone's preferences, which is rarely anyone's idea of good.

## 3. Content takes longer than you think

Nearly every project that slips, slips on content. Text, photography, product details, and legal copy consistently take longer than clients expect, because writing them surfaces questions nobody had answered yet.

Start before the project starts. Even rough drafts beat placeholder text, because design built around fake content breaks when real content arrives — real headlines are longer, real photos are the wrong shape, real product lists have 47 items instead of 3.

## 4. Know what you own

Before you rebuild, establish who controls the domain registrar, the hosting account, the analytics property, and the current site's source code.

This matters at launch, when a missing domain login can hold up a go-live for days. It matters more if you're parting ways with a previous agency. If you can't answer these today, that's the first task — and our [security checklist](/topics/security) covers it in more detail.

## 5. Plan for after launch

A website is not a project with an end date. Content ages, conversion rates drift, competitors move, and security patches arrive whether or not anyone applies them.

Decide up front who owns the site after launch and what "ownership" includes — publishing, updates, monitoring, and measuring whether it still does its job.
