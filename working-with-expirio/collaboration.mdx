Most website projects go wrong in the same place: nobody agreed what "done" meant, or who decides. So before anything else, here is our entire process — every step, every deliverable, and every point where we need a decision from you.

Read it as a contract of expectations. If something here doesn't fit how your team works, tell us at the discovery call, not at step seven.

<Info>
  **Typical total: 7–9 weeks** from discovery call to launch. The variable is rarely us — it's how fast content and approvals come back.
</Info>

## The nine steps

<Steps>
  <Step title="Discovery call">
    A call where we go through the content and goals of the project using our briefing questionnaire.

    **Timeline:** project kickoff · **Output:** Discovery call, meeting
  </Step>

  <Step title="Research, information architecture, wireframe">
    We study your competition, define the problem and the goal, build personas and the user journey, design the information architecture, and produce wireframes and copy.

    **Timeline:** approx. 2 weeks · **Output:** Research, IA, wireframes and copywriting — meeting and approval
  </Step>

  <Step title="Moodboard">
    We prepare a moodboard for the site: style, core colors, fonts, reference websites, special elements.

    **Timeline:** 2–3 days · **Output:** Moodboard — meeting and approval
  </Step>

  <Step title="Homepage design">
    We design the site's main page based on the approved moodboard and the content you've supplied.

    **Timeline:** 2–3 days · **Output:** Homepage — meeting and approval
  </Step>

  <Step title="UI design for the rest of the site">
    We continue designing all remaining pages in line with the approved direction.

    **Timeline:** 1–1.5 weeks · **Output:** Site UI design — meeting and approval
  </Step>

  <Step title="Responsive design">
    We produce the mobile version and the larger breakpoint (1920 px).

    **Timeline:** 2–3 days · **Output:** Responsive design — approval by email
  </Step>

  <Step title="Development">
    We implement the approved designs in the real environment, then test and fine-tune the site's functionality and appearance.

    **Timeline:** 2 weeks · **Output:** Development — internal communication
  </Step>

  <Step title="Handover">
    You take over the site in Webflow (staging) to review it and collect notes for adjustments. Our development team provides video guides so you can run the site comfortably.

    **Timeline:** 2 weeks · **Output:** Handover — meeting and approval
  </Step>

  <Step title="Launch">
    The site is ready to publish, the invoice is paid, and the site can go live. Launch usually happens on a Sunday evening.

    **Timeline:** 1 day · **Output:** Launch — confirmation email
  </Step>
</Steps>

## What we need from you

Six of the nine steps end in a meeting or an approval. That's deliberate — it means nothing large gets built on an assumption nobody checked. It also means the project moves at the speed of your feedback.

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="One decision-maker" icon="user-check">
    Someone who can approve a design without convening a committee. Multiple reviewers are fine; multiple deciders are not.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Content, early" icon="file-text">
    Real copy, images, and product information. Design built on placeholder text usually breaks when the real content arrives.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Timely feedback" icon="clock">
    Approvals gate the next step. A week of silence at step four is a week added to the launch date.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Access" icon="key">
    Domain registrar, current hosting, analytics. Chasing credentials during launch week is the most avoidable delay there is.
  </Card>
</Columns>

## Why launch happens on a Sunday evening

It looks eccentric until you've done it a few times. Sunday evening is the low point of the week for traffic on almost every B2B site. If something needs adjusting after the switch, we're doing it while nobody is looking — rather than at 10am on a Tuesday when your sales team is sending the link to prospects.

## What happens after launch

Launch is not the end of the relationship, and a website is not a project that finishes. Content ages, conversion rates drift, and security patches keep arriving whether or not anyone is watching.
