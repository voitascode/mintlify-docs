The most expensive assumption in web projects is that launch is the finish line. It isn't. It's the point where the site starts aging.

Content goes stale. Plugins drift out of date. A form breaks quietly and nobody notices for six weeks because nobody was watching the enquiry count. Meanwhile the person who "looks after the website" also has a full-time job doing something else.

## What ongoing management actually includes

<Columns cols={2}>
  <Card title="Publishing and content" icon="edit">
    New pages, campaign landing pages, blog posts, job listings. The routine work that keeps the site current.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Technical upkeep" icon="refresh">
    Platform updates, integrations, broken links, page speed. Small things that compound when ignored.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Monitoring" icon="activity">
    Uptime, form submissions, SSL and domain expiry, malware and blocklisting. Systems watching, not people remembering.
  </Card>

  <Card title="Measurement" icon="chart-line">
    Whether the site still does its job — traffic quality, conversion rates, and what changed after each release.
  </Card>
</Columns>

## The ownership question

Every item above needs a name attached. Not a department, not "the agency" — a person who would agree, if asked, that it's their responsibility.

This is the same test as the [security checklist](/topics/security): if a task has no owner, it isn't happening, no matter what the contract says.

<Warning>
  The most common failure mode isn't neglect — it's diffusion. Marketing assumes IT handles updates, IT assumes the agency does, the agency assumes it wasn't in scope. Everyone is reasonable and nothing gets done.
</Warning>

## A realistic minimum

If a full management arrangement isn't on the table, these are the things that genuinely can't be skipped:

- **Automated backups**, stored somewhere other than the site's own server, with a test restore at least once a year
- **Platform and plugin updates** on a schedule, not on memory
- **Expiry alerts** for SSL certificates and domain renewal, sent by a system
- **A monthly look** at form submissions and enquiry volume — the cheapest way to catch a silently broken contact form

Everything beyond that improves the site. These four keep it alive.

## How we handle it

We build on Webflow, which removes a large share of the maintenance surface — no server patching, no plugin ecosystem to track, no PHP version to keep supported. That isn't a claim that nothing needs attention; it's a claim that the attention goes into content and conversion rather than keeping the lights on.
