A website shouldn't be a finished set of pages that only the original supplier can work with.

After launch you need to change text and photographs, publish new content, build landing pages and respond to whatever the company is dealing with right now. Ideally without waiting on a developer for every routine edit.

That's why we don't hand over a Webflow site as a technically finished output. We prepare it as a system your team can keep working with.

## Routine edits you can handle yourself

Day-to-day management of the site doesn't require you to touch code or interfere with the design.

In a simple editing interface, you can:

- change text, links, images and video,
- update contact details and other routine content,
- add articles, case studies, job listings or team members,
- manage content stored in the CMS,
- prepare changes for review and then publish them.

The interface is set up so that content can be changed without accidentally affecting the structure or visual style of the page.

At handover we show you how to work with the site, and we prepare guides for the tasks you'll be doing most often.

## New pages from prepared building blocks

Changing text or a photograph is only the basics. Marketing teams often need to create an entire new page as well.

That might be:

- a campaign landing page,
- a page for a new service,
- a product or industry page,
- event registration,
- a comparison page,
- a page for a new market.

In Webflow we can prepare approved templates and a set of reusable components. Your team then assembles new pages from them, much like working with ready-made building blocks.

Sections can be added, moved, hidden or removed. You can adapt their content for a specific purpose while typography, colors, spacing and other brand rules stay intact.

This gives marketing the speed it needs while protecting the consistency of the site. A routine campaign doesn't have to wait for new design and development, but it also doesn't end up outside the established visual system.

What you can assemble depends on which templates and components we prepare together for your site.

## Changes across the whole site

Some elements appear on dozens of pages — navigation, the footer, contact prompts, announcements or repeated product blocks.

We build these as centrally managed components. When a shared detail needs to change, or their basic form needs adjusting, there's no need to go through every page individually.

We also distinguish between a global change and editing one specific instance. That means you can keep the same call-to-action structure while using different text on each page.

## Multiple languages in one place

If the site has several language versions, you manage them within a single project.

You can switch between languages directly in Webflow and edit:

- page content,
- CMS items,
- images and other media,
- URLs,
- SEO titles and descriptions,
- elements intended for one specific language or market.

The main language forms the shared foundation. Individual language versions can then be translated and adapted for local audiences as needed.

Webflow also offers machine translation. It can speed the work up, but the final text should always be reviewed by someone who understands the language and the market.

The goal is that a routine change across several languages isn't a multi-week technical task. You still need to decide who is responsible for the accuracy and currency of each language version.

## Data alongside the website

Managing a website isn't only publishing. You also need to see what visitors actually use and where they run into problems.

Depending on the setup you choose, we can connect the site to external analytics or use Webflow Analyze. Your team can then track:

- traffic to individual pages,
- where visits come from,
- clicks on important elements,
- completion of defined goals,
- how visitors behave on a page,
- results after publishing a change.

That lets you base further changes on how visitors actually behave. After launching a new landing page, for example, you can see whether people are arriving and whether they continue to the action you want.

Analytics needs to be configured around the site's specific goals. Visit counts alone usually aren't enough.

## Working with the site using AI

Webflow can be connected to compatible AI tools through its official MCP server.

Once appropriate access is granted, AI can help with:

- creating and editing pages,
- working with CMS content,
- updating text and metadata,
- preparing new content variants,
- repetitive edits across the site.

You can then work with the site through ordinary instructions in natural language — via Codex, ChatGPT, Claude or another supported tool.

AI access doesn't replace the rules of managing the site. It still has to be clear who can make changes, who reviews them and what can be published without further approval. We set permissions according to what the team actually needs.

## Access for different team members

Not everyone needs the ability to change the entire website.

In Webflow, people can be assigned different roles based on what they do. One person might only comment on designs, another edits content, and the marketing team assembles new pages from prepared components.

Depending on your needs, roles can be separated along these lines:

- review and commenting,
- editing text, images and the CMS,
- assembling new marketing pages,
- publishing changes,
- managing the design system,
- managing settings for the whole site.

Access can be changed or removed later. The company doesn't have to share one common account, and each person gets only the permissions their work actually requires.

## Who decides about changes

A site being easy to edit doesn't mean everyone should publish anything.

We recommend deciding:

- who is responsible for each part of the content,
- who can create new pages,
- who checks brand consistency,
- who approves legal or specialist information,
- who can publish changes,
- who decides when feedback conflicts.

In a smaller team, one person may cover most of these. In a larger company they may be split between marketing, product, sales and leadership.

What matters is that every area has a specific owner — not just a department, but a person who knows it's their responsibility.

A simple approval process keeps the site fast to work with while preventing its content and style from gradually falling apart.

## Security and technical operation

Webflow handles hosting, platform updates, the SSL certificate and much of the technical operation. There's no server to maintain, no PHP to update and no conventional plugin system to manage.

You do still need to set up access correctly, protect user accounts, review external integrations and know who owns the domain and the other services connected to the site.

Our [security checklist](/topics/security) covers the details.
