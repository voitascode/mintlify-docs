Most B2B content programmes fail the same way: an ambitious start, twelve posts in three months, then a gap that grows until the blog's most recent entry is eighteen months old and quietly damaging your credibility.

The problem is rarely writing ability. It's that nobody decided what the content was *for*.

## Start with the decision, not the keyword

Your buyers make a sequence of decisions before they contact anyone: whether the problem is worth solving, what kind of solution fits, which providers are credible, and whether you specifically are a safe choice.

Content that helps with one of those decisions gets read and remembered. Content written because a keyword tool showed volume usually helps with none of them.

## A cadence you can actually sustain

One good piece a month beats four rushed ones followed by silence. Consistency signals a functioning company; an abandoned blog signals the opposite.

Practical ways to lower the cost per piece:

- **Record instead of writing.** A 20-minute conversation with whoever knows the topic, transcribed and edited, is faster than staring at a blank page.
- **Reuse deliberately.** One substantial piece becomes a newsletter, three social posts, and a section of a sales deck.
- **Write from the sales inbox.** If someone asked it by email, the answer is already half-drafted.
- **Set a floor, not a target.** "One per month, always" beats "one per week, aspirationally."

<Note>
  Publish the date on your articles. Undated content reads as stale to both readers and AI assistants — and dated content that's genuinely current builds more trust than hiding the timeline.
</Note>

## Content and discoverability are linked

Content is what makes a site findable — there's nothing to rank or cite without it. But the causation runs one way: good content can be optimized; optimized thin content stays thin.
