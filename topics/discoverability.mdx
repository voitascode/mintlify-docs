For twenty years, "being found online" meant one thing: ranking on Google. That's still true for most B2B sites, but it's no longer the whole picture. A growing share of buyers now ask an AI assistant instead of running a search — and the rules for showing up in that answer are related to SEO, but not identical.

## SEO: what still matters

Most of what works has been stable for years, which is good news if you've been ignoring the tactics-of-the-month.

## GEO: showing up in AI answers

Generative Engine Optimization is the newer half — being cited when someone asks ChatGPT, Claude, or Google's AI overview about your category.

The mechanics differ from search in one important way: there's no ranked list. The assistant produces a single synthesized answer, and you're either part of it or invisible. There is no page two to be on.

What appears to influence this:

- **Clear, factual, self-contained statements.** Assistants quote content that reads as a direct answer, not content that builds to a point over five paragraphs.
- **Structured, extractable pages.** Headings that match real questions, definitions stated plainly, tables and lists that survive being pulled out of context.
- **Being referenced elsewhere.** Assistants draw on the wider web, so mentions on credible third-party sites matter as much as your own pages.
- **Specificity.** Numbers, dates, and named details get cited. Marketing adjectives don't.

<Note>
  The practical upside: the same writing that works for AI answers also works for humans skimming a page. Clarity isn't a separate optimization track.
</Note>

## What we'd check first

If you want a quick read on where you stand, in order:

1. **Search your own category** the way a buyer would — not by your brand name. Are you anywhere?
2. **Ask an AI assistant** the same question. See who gets named.
3. **Check what your site actually says** about the things buyers ask. Most B2B sites describe what the company does, not what the customer needs to decide.
4. **Run a speed test** on your key pages. Slow pages lose in both channels.

<Warning>
  Discoverability work is slow. Anything promising results in two weeks is either buying links or targeting terms nobody searches.
</Warning>
